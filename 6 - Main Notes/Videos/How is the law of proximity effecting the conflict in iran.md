## [[Law of Proximity]]


- The **law of proximity** in Gestalt psychology says that objects that are close to each other are perceived as belonging together or forming a group.


- According to the law of proximity, things that are close together seem to be more related than things that are farther spaced apart.

###### This is prevalent in UX design, as the designer will likely put elements that are similar close to each other. The user perceives that they are related by the law of proximity. when you play a game. you don't just play one game, you play multiple and play the most proximate game. For example, most people will play the family game, where sibling vie for attention from parents. Parents in turn are playing another game where they want to compete with other parents for social prestige, such as assets, accomplishments, and the accomplishments of their children.

###### In my daily life, I am actively participating in the job game and the school game. I compete with other people for positions in tech roles, and I am also competing in school in order to get good grades (Although, this is not really a competition with others, as I could care less about their scores).

###### The Law of proximity scales, meaning that we participate in the game of making our community the best, to then trying to make our county the best, to making our state the best, to making our country the best.

###### I don't believe we apply this principle when it comes to making the world the best. There are too many countries and too many differences, either with language, religion, skin tone, and other things that will never be able to fully unite us.

- This relates to another one of the Gestalt principles; [[The Law of Similarity]], which states that similar things tend to be grouped together.
