## Crito's arguments

### The reason Crito thinks that [[Socrates]] should escape is because:

* He believes Socrates is endangering the good reputation of his friends
* He need not worry about any of the risks these friends may be running
* he should not worry about the money needed for the escape attempt
* There will be good places to go to, where he can lead an enjoyable life
* He is acting unjustly by not striving to fulfil his obligations to his children
* It amounts to cowardice to accept this situation without resistance.

## The voice of the Laws of Athens

### Escaping would be considered unjust because:

* it would constitute a step towards their destruction
* there is an agreement between him and the Laws, akin to that between a son and his parents and of even greater weight, requiring filial obedience on his part in return for the upbringing they have given him.
* Such obedience is demanded irrespective of the inconvenience and dangers which he may face. He may try to persuade them of the justice of his case, but if he fails it is his duty to obey.


##### Socrates sounds like an authoritarian here. First he claims that the only ones to listen in terms of law and justice are the experts on it; hence lawyers and the government and the police force in the present day. He does not acknowledge that they might get things wrong. He then now says that those laws are absolute, and to break them or go against them is a moral sin. He says you may attempt to persuade them, but if the system is opinion based and people can make rules that convenience them, then they is a slim chance of convincing them. This system is flawed.
