# Euthyphro

##### Author: [[Plato]]


## References


### Notes

The main idea of the story is to introduce the reader to the Socratic method, which is a line of questioning meant to uncover the truth.

Euthyphro is at the courthouse in order to prosecute his father for murder. He claims to know what piety and holiness are.

## Irony in the dialogue

>pg. 13
>"I have naturally become a pupil of yours - 'And presuming, Meletus' I could say 'that you admit that Euthyphro is an authority in such matters'"


>pg. 25
>"because I've already learnt well enough from you what is pious and holy and what is not."


>pg. 19
>"Come now, Euthyphro, my friend, teach me too - make me wiser"

>pg. 28
>"That's because I'm a zealot Euthyphro, zealous for your wisdom"


[[Socrates]] uses irony and attempts to flatter Euthyphro in order to get him to talk and in order to get him to reveal his arguments for deconstruction



## Main points in the argument

>pg. 14
>"Then do you think that there is really a civil war among the gods, and fearful hostility and battles, and so on -the kind of thing described by poets?"

###### This line is meant to show how since the many gods up in the sky have arguments, and since Euthyphro stated that it is the gods who decide what is holy and what is not, then there cannot logically be a single definition of holiness, since the gods are disagreeing constantly


>pg. 16
>"If you and I were in a dispute about which of the two numbers is greater, would our dispute about this turn us into enemies, and make us angry with each other? Or should we quickly settle our differences by resorting to arithmetic?"

###### This set up is to show that with matters such as measurements, we can easily determine what the exact size us, but with holiness, since the gods decide what is holy, there is no way to measure if something is holy or not, since there are different "measurements" for it


>pg. 20
>"is the holy approved by the gods because its holy, or is it holy because it is approved"

###### This is a circular point, since if the gods approve something then it must be holy, but at the same time, the subject in talks must be holy to be approved, so where does holiness come from?

>pg. 21
>"If something is coming to be so or is being affected, then its not the case that it gets to be so because its coming to be so, but, that its coming to be so because it gets to be so;"

>pg. 24
>"where is found fear, there is also found shame, but where is found shame, fear is also found, though shame is not found everywhere where fear is"

###### This quote is used to highlight the subsets of emotions. Shame is a subset of fear. The same way that justness is a subset of holiness as argued by Socrates

>pg. 25
>"We say that not everybody knows hot to look after horses, only the groom, right?"

>pg. 25
>"Nor indeed does everybody know how to look after dogs; only the kennel master"

##### These two are meant to symbolize that the kennel master and the grooms are the only ones that can properly look after dogs and horses respectively. 


###### [[Euthyphro]] says that holiness is that part of justice which looks after the gods, which Socrates thinks implies that the gods are improved by holiness

###### This also begs the question of why the gods would need to be improved by holiness, if they themselves should be holy already. Why would the unholy be deciding on what is holy??


>pg. 29
>"when you yourself are far more skilled than Daedalus"

###### Daedalus crafted mythical statues that moved and were considered slippery, just how Euthyphro is being slippery in the way he defines what is holy and 'divinely approved', which Socrates says are not the same.
