## Main notes

pg 43
"The oracle of Apollo has declared that he is the wisest of men, and he has been trying to find men wiser than he is as a part of his search for the god's meaning"

This passage goes to show that even after being granted the title of the wisest man by the renowned oracle, he will still question this and seek the truth out for himself. This shows his true nature.


pg 43
"What kind  wisdom do I mean? Human wisdom I suppose"

I found this passage to be interesting because he does not state true wisdom. this is important for Phaedo, as there he goes in depth on the soul and claims that true wisdom cannot be attained while attached to a physical body that is tainted by emotions and desires.

pg 44
"But he thinks that he knows something which he does not know,  whereas i am quite conscious of my ignorance"

This is peak Socrates right here! He never claims to be wise or to know the truth of everything. This is why he was excited to talk to Euthyphro or to talk to any man that claims to be "wise". Socrates is always trying to orient himself towards the truth, using logic and reasoning and questioning everything


##### Comparative Ignoramus
This is essentially being uneducated in comparison to experts in a specific field


pg 47

"there is a pestilential busybody called Socrates who fills young peoples heads with the wrong ideas. If you ask them what he does, and what he teaches that has this effect, they have no answer"


This relates to the elites wanting power. If everyone has power and knowledge, then they have ability to question the ones in power, and therefore, the elites would not be very powerful. They put a target on Socrates in order to eradicate thought and questioning that might expose them.


pg 49

"Take the case of horses; do you believe that those who improve them make up the whole of mankind, and that there is only one person who has a bad effect on them? Or is the truth just the opposite?"

Context: Before this, [[Socrates]] questions Meletus on whether the young people should be exposed to good influences, in which Meletus answers yes. Socrates asks him who makes the young good, which he answers the laws, but who makes the laws? That is the jurymen. 

Socrates gets Meletus to say that everyone in the court house has the ability to improve the youth, except Socrates, which is why he brings up the example of the horses, since not everyone has this power

##### In my opinion, Socrates does not have the power to do this either. Who decides what is good or bad? Socrates argues that there are some that have the ability to do good, but how can he know he is one of these people


pg 50

"Either I have not a bad influence, or it is unintentional; so in either case what you claim is true."


Socrates basically says if the students were harmed, then they would have harmed him back which they did not do. If the bad influence was unintentional, then the correct procedure is to not summon the culprit before the court. If his ideas were harmful, then people would not keep showing up to listen to him on the streets of Athens, were his lectures were free



pg 51
"Is it that I teach people to believe in some gods (which implies that I myself believe in gods, and am not a complete atheist, and so not guilty on that score), but in different gods from those recognized by the State"


There is a contradiction here. The charges that Meletus brought up  the beginning was that Socrates was worshipping false gods, but in the line after which this quote was copied from, Meletus says that he believes that Socrates does not believe in gods altogether! Which is absurd for what he claims.



p 54
"You are mistaken , my friend, if you think that a man who is worth anything ought to spend his time weighing up the prospects of life or death"


This is the core of who Socrates is. He is someone who wans to get to the truth of things. If this means that he must die for the truth, the so be it. Socrates would rather do that than to be a shell of a man who doesn't question anything. As a philosopher, this is what he is destined to do.


pg 55
"That to be afraid of death is only another form of thinking that one is wise when one is not;"

I like this passage because it is purely Socratic. Think about it. We do not know what comes after death. Whether there is a heaven or a hell,  and since we do not know the truth, the Socrates argues that fearing it is thinking that you are wiser than others. The logic is airtight. Why be afraid of something that you don't know about. For all we know, there is no more pain and suffering.


pg 56
" Are you not ashamed that you give attention to acquiring as much money as possible?"

"and give no attention or thought to truth and understanding  and the perfection of your soul"


This of course will relate to [[Phaedo]], where Socrates goes more in depth on what he believes happens after death. It is peculiar that Socrates mentions the "perfection of your soul", since he argues that attunement is bullshit, but this literally sounds like attunement. If this were reorientation, then would the soul be pure, and the body un pure and unable to understand it?


pg 58
"If i had tried long ago to engage in politics, I should have long ago have lost my life"

"The true champion of justice, if he intends to survive even for a short time, must necessarily confine himself to private life and leave politics alone"


Its funny because politics and politicians are essentially liars still in 2026. Who knew that being in positions of power would cause you to abuse the justice system and benefit yourself and others in your circle. Clear connection to our modern day and age.


pg 63

"he ought to inform them of the facts and convince them by argument"

This is an important takeaway. In Ancient Athens, people would usually beg for mercy, either for themselves (obviously) or their family. Socrates refuses and basically says "no man fight me with facts or don't fight me at all". He believed that justice was the truth, and the through justice, there could be order in a society. This feeds into what he says in [[Crito]], when he refuses to escape because he wants to do things within the system. He believe that escaping is something that could lead to the destruction of the system he wants to uphold so badly.


pg 65
"Nothing could be more appropriate for such a person that free dining in the Prytaneum. He deserves it much more than any victor in the races at Olympia".


Socrates is being truthful here when he is asked what he deserves for the so called "crimes" he has committed. He requested to dine at one of the most fancy restaurants in Athens. He truly believe that his time speaking the truth, upholding justice, and teaching the people of Athens to think critically was a great service and one which he deserves a reward, not a punishment. It is what is deserved
