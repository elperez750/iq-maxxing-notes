## Book 1

In Book 1, some of the main ideas being presented is the idea of [[Individual Justice]]. The main actors here are of course [[Socrates]], [[Glaucon]], [[Ademantis]], [[Thrysimicas]]. Some of the arguments brought up is whether justice should be done to enemies as well as friends. [[Thrysimicas]] comes in and basically opens up Pandoras Box and says that justice is in the hands of those who rule. Even in the examples and the ideal city that Socrates sets up, it seems obvious that he is also doing the same thing that [[Thrysimicas]] said by deciding what is good and what is bad for a society to consume. The main argument that [[Socrates]] makes in book 1 is the fact that for working citizens, they only want to get the best of the ignorant man, and not the wise man. In Michael Sugrue's video, he explains the logic. When a doctor walks in to another doctor performing a surgery for the heart in a manner that is correct and will not endanger the patient, then he will not try to get the better of that man. But, if there is a quack doctor who is trying to perform a surgery, then the real doctor will try to get the better of him since he will have done harm to the patient. This is the argument made in order to prove that the ruling class of a city do not attempt to get the better out of the people living in it.

###### To break this down, the definition coming from Polemarchus is that Justice is doing good to friends and harm to enemies, but Socrates rightfully probes this definition.
